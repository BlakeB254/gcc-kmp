Background

What We're Building

Global Community Cleaners is a technology platform that turns neighborhood cleanup into verified, GPS-tracked, AI-classified civic data. Community members report dirty spots through our mobile app, verify each other's reports, clean blocks, and earn points and rewards for their work. Every action is documented with before-and-after photos, GPS coordinates, and AI-powered waste classification.

Launched in North Lawndale on Chicago's West Side, GCC is designed to scale across all 77 of Chicago's community areas. Our data is structured for the Chicago Data Portal (SODA-compatible, 25+ fields per report), creating a new layer of verified environmental intelligence that doesn't exist anywhere else.

But technology alone doesn't clean neighborhoods. We need a network of partners who provide the resources, rewards, and infrastructure that keep community workers engaged and coming back to their blocks. That's where you come in.

Data Partnership

The Chicago Data Portal Opportunity

Chicago's 311 system captures complaints. But there's no structured way to verify that reported problems were actually resolved, who resolved them, or what the area looked like before and after. GCC fills that gap.

Our data is already structured for the Chicago Data Portal—SODA-compatible, with 25+ fields per community report, including GPS coordinates, before/after photo URLs, AI waste classification, and resolution timestamps. We've built the export API. We're ready to integrate.

Our proposal to the City: A 90-day free pilot providing verified cleanup data for North Lawndale. If the data proves valuable, GCC becomes a consulting and data licensing partner for the City's environmental intelligence layer.

This is a partnership that turns volunteer cleanups into structured civic data—the kind of data the City needs for budgeting, aldermanic accountability, and federal reporting. If you know anyone at the Department of Technology or the Department of Environment, that introduction could be transformative.

Academic Partners

University Research Partnerships

GCC generates a novel dataset that doesn't exist anywhere else in civic technology—verified, GPS-tagged, AI-classified environmental data collected by community members at street level. This opens research opportunities across multiple disciplines.

Research Area Opportunity What GCC Provides
Urban Studies Neighborhood cleanliness as a proxy for investment, equity, and gentrification patterns across 77 community areas Longitudinal cleanliness scores, per-area trend data
Computer Science / AI On-device waste classification accuracy, LiDAR volumetric estimation, anti-fraud detection models Training data, model performance benchmarks, edge AI deployment case study
Public Policy Impact of gamification on civic participation; comparing community-driven vs. government-driven cleanup Points/badge engagement data, 311 comparison dataset
Behavioral Economics How incentive structures (points, badges, prize tiers) affect sustained community engagement over time Gamification interaction data, retention metrics, reward redemption patterns
Data Science Predictive models for waste accumulation, optimal cleanup routing, resource allocation 25+ field SODA-compatible API, historical spot data, geographic clustering
Environmental Science Waste composition analysis across community areas, seasonal patterns, illegal dumping hotspots 12-category AI classification data, before/after photo pairs, affected area geometry

Chicago is home to some of the strongest urban research programs in the country. GCC's work aligns naturally with programs like the University of Chicago's Urban Labs, the Harris School of Public Policy, the Data Science Institute, and the Mansueto Institute for Urban Innovation—all of which study exactly the kind of community-level data GCC generates. Similar alignment exists with UIC's Great Cities Institute, Northwestern's Institute for Policy Research, and DePaul's Chaddick Institute for Metropolitan Development.

What we need from academic partners: Student researchers for data analysis and model improvement, a credibility signal that strengthens grant applications, and connections to urban planning faculty and civic tech networks.

What universities get: A novel dataset for publications, a real-world AI/ML deployment case study, and a community engagement research platform operating in one of America's most-studied cities.
