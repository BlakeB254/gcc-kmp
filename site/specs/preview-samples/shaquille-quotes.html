"And I had gotten into looking into this broken windows theory, you know. …there's this broken window theory that just like that boggles my mind. This theory was brought up by James Wilson and Gregory Killings [Kelling] in 1982. And the theory was that if you were in a community with one broken window, it'll possibly lead to two and then three and then more. And then somebody would eventually break into the house and then the house next door would eventually face the same consequences if nobody was living in it. And then eventually the people who live on the block would want to move away from the block… But that leaves an element of vacant land. And in Chicago, y'all see it all day, blocks and blocks and blocks of vacant land, abandoned houses. And those are the conditions of a war zone. If I was to go get you in a plane and take you over to Gaza where they dropping bombs and stuff right now, those are the same conditions. You see broken windows. You see land that's crumbled, stuff on the ground. Only thing we're not missing is the build that's just crumbled on the ground in our communities. And, you know, I work for Divvy, so I drive around the city all day. So it's not no part of the city that you can sit here and tell me about — from the west side, south side, east side or up north side. And it just boggles my mind, like the unjust treatment of these communities… Then I had to come back to the North Lawndale community and I'm walking past abandoned houses, boards on the windows, broken windows, trash everywhere on the floor."
