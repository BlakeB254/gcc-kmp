Sixty years and two blocks

Hi Daisy — this is the first newsletter from Global Community Cleaners. Here is why we exist, what we are doing, and what's next.

On January 26, 1966, Dr. Martin Luther King Jr. carried his bags up to a third-floor walk-up at 1550 South Hamlin Avenue in North Lawndale and started what he called the “action phase” of the Chicago Freedom Movement — the campaign that brought him here to focus on housing conditions and economic opportunity in Chicago neighborhoods. The campaign produced the Summit Agreement, which fed directly into the 1968 Fair Housing Act.

Today, Global Community Cleaners — the organization sending this email — is headquartered at 1844 South Harding, 2nd Floor, a six-block walk from the apartment Dr. King moved into.

Sixty years. Two blocks of distance. Same neighborhood, same work.

What “broken windows” actually proved

Most people know the phrase. Almost no one has read the modern research.

The original 1982 essay by Wilson and Kelling argued that visible disorder invites bigger problems. The intuition was right that physical conditions matter. The newer research has refined what kind of attention to those conditions actually moves the needle.

The lesson: invest in the people on the block, and the block follows. Sociologist Robert Sampson's Chicago research shows that neighborhoods scoring high on collective efficacy — neighbors stepping in for each other — run roughly 40% lower crime rates, even when other variables look similar on paper.

The Branas Trial · Philadelphia · 2018

Greening 541 vacant lots in a randomized citywide trial:

  • 29.1% drop in nearby gun assaults
  • 41.5% fewer residents reporting depression
  • 68.7% drop for residents below the poverty line
  • $7.43 tax-revenue return per public dollar spent (Wharton, 2023)

Branas et al., PNAS 2018 · South et al., JAMA Network Open 2018 · Moyer et al., AJPH 2019

Cleaning blocks is one of the highest-ROI neighborhood interventions ever measured — for safety, for mental health, for property values. And it works best when it is done by the people who live there.

The neighborhood we are working in

The data on North Lawndale is not subtle.

Life expectancy gap: 87.3 years in the Loop. 66.6 in adjacent West Garfield Park. A 20-year gap inside the same city.

CDPH, 2023 Life Expectancy Data Brief (Sept 2025)

Citywide gap: Black Chicagoans died 10.6 years earlier than non-Black Chicagoans in 2023.

Violent crime: roughly 3× the citywide rate — the community is on the official list of areas “most affected by violence.”

UIC Great Cities Institute, 2022

This is the gap a $100 block-cleaning fee is built to close.

Meet the founder, in his own words

Shaquille Tate was born at Mt. Sinai Hospital in North Lawndale and grew up on the blocks running from 15th Street to Ridgeway. He drives for Divvy across the entire city for his day job, which means he sees what every Chicago neighborhood looks like from the curb. Asked on a 2026 radio interview how the idea found him, he answered:

“I had to come back to the North Lawndale community, and I'm walking past abandoned houses — boards on the windows, broken windows, trash everywhere on the floor.”

He had been reading about the broken-windows theory. He knew the academic framing. He was looking for a different lever:

“What if somebody could get all these kids to clean up their block? And if they were to clean up their block, to get paid to clean up their block.”

Then the line that became the entire model:

“I'm employing elders and I'm employing youth and I'm paying them a hundred dollars to clean up their blocks. I want to start with a 10-block radius… This is more than just picking up trash.”

He's right. It is about cleaning, but it is also about cash, dignity, ownership, and the simple fact that the people best equipped to take care of a block are the people who live on it — if someone is willing to pay them like the work matters. Because it does.

How GCC actually works

$100 per block. 100% goes to the resident doing the work. GCC's overhead is funded separately.

One participant per block. Gate to crest, both sides, including the alley.

Elders and youth. Both. The model is intergenerational by design.

Same-day pay. Finish the work, get paid.

Before-and-after photos. Sponsors receive proof. Block status is logged in our system.
