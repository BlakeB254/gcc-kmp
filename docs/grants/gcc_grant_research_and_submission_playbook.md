## 5. Reusable narrative blocks

### Short mission paragraph

Global Community Cleaners creates paid, resident-led neighborhood cleaning opportunities that put money directly into the hands of local workers while making blocks cleaner, safer, and more welcoming. In Chicago’s North Lawndale launch area, GCC connects sponsors and residents through a transparent $100-per-block model, verified by before/after photos and GPS-enabled workflows.

### Environmental / public-health problem statement

Litter and illegal dumping are not just aesthetic issues: they affect walkability, health, stormwater systems, community pride, and perceptions of safety. GCC addresses these problems by combining direct cleanup, local income generation, transparent sponsorship, and photo-verified accountability. The model is designed to build a repeatable neighborhood stewardship infrastructure rather than a one-day volunteer event.

### Economic mobility statement

GCC pays residents for visible, measurable neighborhood improvement. Workers can claim nearby blocks, clean on flexible schedules, document the work, and receive payment after approval. This creates low-barrier income opportunities while building trust, public-space stewardship, and a pipeline for future green jobs and civic leadership.

### Transparency statement

GCC’s block sponsorship promise is simple: when a sponsor funds a $100 block, the full $100 goes to the resident who cleans that block. GCC seeks grants and corporate partnerships to cover platform, training, insurance, equipment, verification, and administrative costs so worker pay is not reduced by platform fees.

## 6. Opportunity-specific preparation notes

### NFWF Five Star

- Avoid presenting the project as only litter pickup. Tie GCC work to **water quality, watershed stewardship, public open-space restoration, outreach/education, and community partnerships**.
- Use NFWF-friendly metrics: pounds of trash/debris removed or prevented, volunteers/participants, people reached, trees/native plants if included, sites monitored.
- Add at least three partner letters: a local community organization, a technical/environmental partner, and a public land/site partner.

### Illinois EPA 319(h) / SCALE

- Complete **GATA** and federal identifiers before the NOFO opens.
- Build a recurring cleanup calendar and track historical participation because SCALE prioritizes established recurring stream/lakeshore cleanup groups.
- Connect target blocks to storm drains, waterways, and watershed plans.

### City of Chicago CDG

- Treat CDG as a capital project, not an operations grant.
- Strongest GCC concepts: training/equipment hub, community stewardship center, storage and dispatch space, worker support location, or a mixed-use partnership facility.
- Prepare site control, construction scope, architect/contractor estimates, budget sources/uses, job creation, community benefits, and long-term operating plan.

### Chicago Community Trust

- For sponsorships, package an event: “North Lawndale Clean Blocks Launch Day,” “Roosevelt Road Sponsor Walk,” or “Clean Blocks + Economic Mobility Convening.”
- For larger opportunities, emphasize equity, economic mobility, resident leadership, and community wellbeing.

### Corporate/foundation asks

- Use a two-tier ask: (1) direct block sponsorships that fund workers and (2) operating/capacity grants that pay for training, verification, insurance, equipment, technology, and evaluation.
- Prepare named packages: 10 blocks ($1,000), 25 blocks ($2,500), 100 blocks ($10,000), corridor sponsorship, employee volunteer day, matching-gift challenge.

## 7. Research caveats and maintenance plan

Grant deadlines and eligibility change frequently. The database should be reviewed every **two weeks** while GCC is actively fundraising, and immediately before submission. For each opportunity, verify the official funder page, download the current NOFO/RFP, and save a PDF copy in the grant folder.

Maintenance workflow:

1. Add each opportunity to a CRM or spreadsheet with owner, deadline, next action, and fit score.
2. Assign one person to weekly checks of Chicago, Illinois, EPA/Grants.gov, NFWF, Chicago Community Trust, and corporate portals.
3. Keep a “submitted applications” folder with final narrative, budget, attachments, and confirmation receipt.
4. After every submission, save funder feedback and update the reusable narrative.
