Who we are, what 20 neighbors already cleaned, and how a gift of any size helps · Sept 2026

Pay the neighbor. Clean the block. Prove it on a phone. Then scale it.

If you have never heard of us, start here. Global Community Cleaners (GCC) is a Chicago charity. We pay a person who lives on a dirty city block $100 to clean that block. Every dollar of that $100 is their paycheck — we keep none of it. A free iPhone app (Android next) lets them claim a paid block, walk both sidewalks, photograph the work, and get paid. This page is the whole story: who we are, what already happened on 20 streets in North Lawndale (a West Side neighborhood), and how your gift becomes a paycheck or keeps the charity running.

First gift-in-kind, and what cash still has to cover. Fields of Dreams Chicago — a North Lawndale youth group that meets at North Lawndale College Prep — gave our first in-kind gift on May 13, 2026 (~$500): 3 boxes of gloves, 3 shovels, 4 dustpan-broom sets, 2 push brooms, 30-pack of pickup sticks, dropped at the UIC Neighborhood Center. Still needed: rakes, contractor bags, high-visibility vests, wheelbarrows, a pressure washer, replacement gloves.

What happens after you give. A signed-up neighbor claims the block in the app, walks both sidewalks, photographs the work. We check the photos, the location stamp (GPS — the same system car maps use), and the time. Then $100 is sent through Stripe (the payment company many online stores use) — usually approved in 15–30 minutes, in their bank in 1–2 days.

Why a cleaner, paid block is more than looks — the research, in plain language

  • Philadelphia vacant-lot study (Branas and colleagues, University of Pennsylvania): randomly cleaning and greening lots cut gun assaults by 29% and depression reports by 42% (69% among lower-income residents). Cities recouped about $7.43 in public value per dollar of greening.
  • Chicago neighborhood study (Robert Sampson, University of Chicago): on blocks where neighbors actually look out for one another, violent crime is about 40% lower than on similar blocks where they do not. Paying a neighbor to care for their own street is that idea with a paycheck and a photo record.
  • This neighborhood. Chicago Department of Public Health: people in the Loop live to about 87; people in West Garfield Park (next door) to about 67 — a 21-year gap. Black Chicagoans died 10.6 years younger than the city average (2023). UIC Great Cities Institute: North Lawndale violent crime is about 3× the city rate. A $100 block wage will not close those gaps alone. It is the smallest honest unit of the same fight.
