Sixty years. Two blocks of distance.
Same neighborhood. Paid work. Verified streets.

On January 26, 1966, Dr. Martin Luther King Jr. carried his bags up to 1550 S. Hamlin Ave and opened the Chicago Freedom Movement’s “action phase” — housing and economic opportunity on these same West Side blocks. The Summit Agreement that followed fed the 1968 Fair Housing Act. Today Global Community Cleaners NFP sits at 1844 S. Harding Ave, 2nd Floor — a six-block walk. We are not a volunteer litter day. We are a resident-powered cleanup economy: sponsors fund a block at $100, a neighbor claims it, GPS + photos + timestamps prove it, Stripe pays 100% of that $100 to the person who did the work. GCC turns trash to data. The stack that makes that true was built in-neighborhood.

What the technology actually feels like in a resident’s hand

Every integration exists because a human on a North Lawndale block has to finish a job and get paid. We do not pitch “AI.” We pitch a paycheck that can be audited.

1. Claim a funded blockLive map of sponsored routes. $100 shows on the card (e.g. 1500 S Pulaski). Resident taps, owns the block. Kotlin Multiplatform app, iOS live, Android next.
2. Two-lap guided routeWalk both sides of the street. Coverage map shows what’s done. Bags and gear in hand. This is the job, not a scavenger hunt.
3. Scan-verify (AR + LiDAR)Before/after is not a selfie. ARKit poses + LiDAR depth + 1Hz GPS. On-device litter classifier (CoreML; TACO + our own crops) names bottles, bags, bulk dump in real time.
4. Get paid on proofPhotos + GPS + timestamps trip Stripe. 100% of the $100. Typical 15–30 min after approval, 24–48h to bank. July field bugs: we photographed by hand and paid anyway. v1.0.9 fixed the flow.
5. Heatmap + spots + bountiesAnyone can pin a mess. Neighbors verify. A $10+ bounty turns a dump into a paid job. Points, badges, leaderboards reward scans of never-seen or stale blocks.
6. Digital twin (accountability)158 sessions · 919+ R2 artifacts. Depth + pose + GPS fuse per-block 3D. Metric-accurate vs GPS. A sponsor’s $100 buys a cleanup; the twin is how nobody has to take our word for it.

Pipeline: iPhone (KMP + ARKit + on-device ID) → Cloudflare R2 → Ktor on DGX → Neon/Next → Stripe. Android: UIC-sponsored device so this neighborhood is not locked to iOS. Graphene Gangway keeps the stack in community hands.

Issue #1 data, on the map — pay for the bag, then stop the next pile

Pattern 1 · Heat along vacant-lot edges. The red bloom is not “people don’t care.” It is wind-driven fenceline accumulation — light debris funnels into chain-link and sits. Zoomed-out heat names the cluster; the photo is what a $100 block actually faces. Data fix: windbreak plantings or screen fabric on the worst lines + a cleanup cadence after wind events — not infinite bags.
Same pattern, street scale. This is the field evidence behind the heat. A forever-loop of $100 blocks here is the wrong spend. A $40 screen + a post-wind route is the right one. We can name the fences. We have the GPS.
Pattern 2 · Corridors without cans. Roosevelt is the named corridor (green = UIC-funded cleaned blocks). Hotspot density is higher on pedestrian stretches with no public trash can for blocks at a time. Data fix: bins at high-foot-traffic corners — city ask, property owner, or a sponsored-bin add-on. A $40 can beats another $100 forever.
Same pattern, vacant-lot dump on a walkway. Construction debris lands where there is no receptacle and no steward. The map tells us which corners; the photo tells us what a bin + a paid resident would stop. We clean and we hand the City / UIC Great Cities / block clubs a punch list.

Why the academic record says invest in the people on the block

Branas RCT · Penn

Cluster-randomized lots in Philadelphia (PNAS, JAMA Network Open): greened vs trash-removal-only vs control. 29.1% drop in gun assaults. 41.5% fewer depression reports. 68.7% for low-income residents. Municipal tax ROI $7.43 per public dollar on greening.

Sampson · Chicago

Collective efficacy — whether neighbors feel any agency over their block — predicts ~40% lower crime even when income, race, and visible disorder match. A paid neighbor with a verified before/after is efficacy with a receipt.

This neighborhood

20.7-year life-expectancy gap (Loop 87.3 vs West Garfield Park 66.6). Black Chicagoans died 10.6 years earlier (CDPH 2023). North Lawndale ~3× city violent-crime rate (UIC Great Cities Institute, 2022). We are the block-wage layer of that same fight.

Relationships the $100 starts — and the neighbor who equipped the first crews

Fields of Dreams Chicago — first in-kind gift, May 13, 2026, ~$500. Founder James Fields left 3 boxes of gloves, 3 shovels, 4 stainless dustpan-and-broom sets, 2 push brooms, and a 30-pack of pickup sticks at the UIC Neighborhood Center. They run Saturday Neighborhood Nurturers at North Lawndale College Prep, serving Black and Latinx youth 11–15. While GCC pays this generation to steward blocks, Fields of Dreams is raising the next one. Still not covered by in-kind: rakes, contractor bags, high-vis vests, wheelbarrows, pressure washer, replacement gloves — plus every dollar that is not a physical tool (Stripe, insurance, phones, Android, reconstruction compute, people).
What a paycheck buys besides a clean curb. The 20 UIC residents are not a one-off crew. They are the first people we can call for events, deeper cleans, youth/elder pairing, and Graphene Gangway tech sessions. Fast income in a neighborhood where living-wage work is scarce is how you earn the right to talk about “opportunities.” Shaquille’s bet: if the founder still has to stack part-time jobs, the kids watching do not believe community work is a career. Operating support that pays him to go full-time is a signal, not a perk.
