From Street Scans to a Living Digital Twin

How every walk with the GCC app becomes lasting 3D evidence of a neighborhood's condition — and how we maximize every byte of it.

Every scan is captured once, used many times

When a GCC worker scans a block, their iPhone records far more than a photo. The app continuously spools HEVC video, LiDAR depth keyframes, and a per-frame pose sidecar — the exact camera position, orientation, and lens parameters for every frame, thirty times a second. Nothing is discarded: raw capture uploads to durable cloud storage the moment a chunk completes, so the field work is done once and the data keeps paying out.

One capture, four products

From a single walk we derive: (1) coverage — which 10-meter cells of ground were verifiably seen, at what quality; (2) observations — geo-located litter and condition findings that live until the area is rescanned; (3) photoreal 3D — Gaussian-splat reconstructions of the actual street; and (4) geometry — measurable point clouds and meshes derived from LiDAR. Multiple workers' sessions of the same place are registered together, so the model of each block sharpens as the community keeps scanning.

Why this matters

Cities describe problems in text. GCC shows them — before, during, and after cleanup — with survey-grade spatial context. As reconstruction methods improve, the archived raw scans can be re-processed into ever-better models without anyone walking the block again: the dataset appreciates in value over time.

The geographic scan cache — a health record per location

Every point on the ground maps deterministically to a ~3–10 m grid cell ID — one shared formula across the phone app, the servers, and the storage paths, so a cell is the same cell everywhere. Each cell accumulates a compact health record:

  • Coverage tier — seen (sighted at distance), grazed (scanned nearby), or close (verified up-close with quality-gated tracking). Tiers only upgrade; an indoor false-positive purge can retract them. The append-only event log is the source of truth — the summary is always recomputable.
  • Freshness — first/last scanned timestamps drive "needs rescan" prioritization and the fog-of-war map.
  • Observation lifecycle — a finding (e.g. dumped debris) displays until its cell is rescanned, then automatically resolves to still-present (confirmed by the new scan) or cleaned (superseded) — no manual bookkeeping.
  • 3D status — which reconstruction version covers the cell, and whether it has met the multi-session activation gate (independent scans agreeing) that marks it canonical.

Trust & integrity choices

  • Provenance-first: every derived asset links back to its source sessions; nothing is untraceable.
  • Append-only everything: raw scans, coverage events, and reconstruction versions never overwrite history.
  • Evidence validation: visual evidence must be geographically consistent (within 150 m) with the report it claims to document.
  • Cross-session agreement: a location's 3D model is only promoted to canonical once ≥3 independent sessions register consistently.

Open-Data Integration

Building on Chicago's Data — and Giving Back Eyes

The city's premises, our foundation

Our maps are organized the way Chicago organizes itself: by the 77 official community areas, pulled directly from the Chicago Data Portal — the same boundaries that structure every city dashboard. On top of that shared frame we've indexed the entire portal catalog (1,600+ datasets, 626 geo-enabled), so any city dataset — broadband zones, transit, zoning, service requests — can be applied to any of our maps as a live layer in a few clicks. New map projects (a neighborhood initiative, a business district, an infrastructure study) are created through a simple form and inherit the whole system.

The 311 loop: open data that asks for help — and gets it

The city's report is text and a street address. Ours adds timestamped photos, LiDAR geometry, and a photoreal reconstruction — plus a follow-up mechanism the city lacks: every rescan of that ground updates the finding to still-present or cleaned, automatically. That capture-rate number, per neighborhood, is a new civic metric no one else publishes.

Where this goes next

  • Denser canonical models as multi-session activation kicks in across the pilot area.
  • AI world-models (single-image and splat-to-environment generation) to fill unscanned gaps and stylize the cumulative scan map into a seamless, Google-Earth-like experience built from our own ground truth.
  • More sources: county/state open data, OpenStreetMap, transit feeds, and partner datasets — each just another layer on maps anyone can create.
